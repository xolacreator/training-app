# Fitstop programming, 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-12

**Three weeks. Block D (2026-09-14 to 2026-12-05) is not yet supplied and is the block that
actually runs into the race - see section 7.**

**A transcription of one athlete's gym programming, for scheduling around. Not doctrine.**

Read the next three paragraphs before using anything below them.

This document exists so a coaching system can see what an athlete is *already doing* six days
a week, and program the gap rather than duplicating the overlap. It is a faithful record of
three consecutive weeks of Fitstop class programming as supplied by the athlete on 2026-09-07.

**It is not evidence and it is not doctrine.** Training Intelligence Platforms already ruled on
exactly this question. `OB-42` flags the training app's existing `strength.fitstop_method`
domain, which encoded one gym's 12-week block as `sourceTier: methodology`, and calls it
"a category error, not an accuracy error - it is a personal program in the knowledge layer."
`DR-0021` draws the same line for elite practice: attribution is not verification, and a
program is not a finding. **Nothing in this document may be graded, cited as support for a
claim, or presented to any athlete as what training should look like.** It describes what one
gym prescribed to one member on three specific weeks.

**It is also not a general Fitstop description.** Three weeks is three weeks. Fitstop programs
in blocks and this sample lands on a 1RM testing wave, which is a distinctive phase and not
representative of the rest of the year. Treat every derived pattern below as provisional until
more weeks arrive.

---

## 1. The weekly template

Six sessions, Monday to Saturday. The day types are stable across all three weeks; only the
Friday format moved (in week 3 it becomes `2 x 15 min + finisher`).

| Day | Type | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | **Perform** | 4 x 9 min |
| Tuesday | **Lift** | 9 \| 18 \| 9 min |
| Wednesday | **Condition** | varies: 35 min cap, 3 x 12 min, 36 min AMRAP |
| Thursday | **Perform** | 9 \| 18 \| 9 min (wk 1: 8 \| 16 \| 8 + finisher) |
| Friday | **Lift** | 9 \| 18 \| 9 min (wk 3: 2 x 15 min + finisher) |
| Saturday | **Sweat** | 4 x 9 min, 5 x 7 min, then **Game Day** 5 x 14 min |

Structure notes that matter for scheduling:

- Most sessions are **teams of 3, max 9 per station**. Wednesday and some Saturday work is
  pairs (max 12) or individual. Session length is fixed by the class, not the athlete.
- Every running prescription carries a **NON-RUNNING alternative** (kick throughs, mountain
  climbers, box step ups, bike/ski/row). Running is optional throughout.
- `WFT` = work for time. `AMRAP` = as many rounds as possible. `E3MOM` / `E4:30MIN` / `E80SEC`
  = every N, on the minute. `YG/IG` = you go, I go. `RIR` = reps in reserve. `S.A.` = single
  arm. `S.L.` = single leg. `FFE` = front foot elevated. `T&G` = touch and go.
  `STOH` = shoulder to overhead.

## 2. The three-week arc

This sample is a **1RM testing wave**, and that is the single most important thing about it.

| | Chin up | Back squat | Bench press | Deadlift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Build to heavy (Mon) | Build to heavy (Tue) | Build to heavy (Thu) | Build to heavy (Fri) |
| **Week 2** | Find your 1RM (Mon) | Find your 1RM (Tue) | Find your 1RM (Thu) | Find your 1RM (Fri) |
| **Week 3** | Build or test (Mon) | Build or test (Tue) | Build or test (Mon/Thu) | Build or test (Thu) |

Week 1 carries explicit trainer ramp examples, stated as "Members Goal weight for their 1RM
next week is X; goal this week is just under that":

- **Back squat, goal 80 kg:** 8@30, 5@40, 3@50, 2@60, 1@70, 1@72.5, 1@75, 1@77.5
- **Bench press, goal 60 kg:** 8@20, 5@40, 3@45, 2@50, 1@52.5, 1@55, 1@57.5
- **Deadlift, goal 100 kg:** 8@40, 5@60, 3@70, 2@80, 1@90, 1@92.5, 1@95, 1@97.5

Week 2 adds **testing time guides** so the whole class hits singles together, e.g. back squat
`0:00-2:00-4:00-6:00-9:00-12:00-15:00`, chin up `0:00-1:30-3:00-5:00-7:00`.

**There is a recurring benchmark.** Monday of every week contains, unchanged:

> **2 KM TIME TRIAL - INDIVIDUAL.** 2 km row/ski, or 5 km bike.

That is a repeated, dated, comparable measurement in the athlete's own week. It is the most
useful single number in this document for any system trying to track fitness over time. Note
carefully: **it is a row/ski/bike test, not a run test.** It must never be used to set running
paces.

## 4. What this covers against HYROX, and what it leaves

The eight demands of a HYROX race, held against three weeks of the above. `covered` means
trained at something near race demand; `partial` means touched but not at race demand.

| Demand | State | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength | **covered** | Two Lift days a week on squat, bench, deadlift and chin up, on an explicit 1RM wave. This is the strongest part of the week. |
| SkiErg and row | **covered** | Present in almost every Perform, Condition and Sweat session, in cals rather than metres. |
| Wall balls | **partial** | Plate thrusters, ball squat cleans, KB thrusters and ball reverse lunges are adjacent patterns. No 100-rep wall ball set. |
| Carries | **partial** | 50 m S.A. KB suitcase carry appears once. Nothing at 200 m farmers or 100 m sandbag lunge distance. |
| Running off a station | **partial** | Running sits inside circuits (200 m after each Sevens round, 300 m between rows) which is genuinely compromised. But it is 100-400 m, not 1 km, and it is always optional. |
| Aerobic running volume | **UNCOVERED** | The longest continuous run in three weeks is **1 km**, once, on 2026-09-03, with a non-running alternative offered. HYROX is 8 km. |
| Sustained hard running | **UNCOVERED** | The only repeated time trial is on a row, ski or bike. There is no threshold running anywhere in the sample. |
| **Sled push and pull** | **UNCOVERED** | **The sled does not appear once in three weeks.** Two race stations, absolute loads, zero exposure. |

**The three gaps are the whole finding**, and they are the reason this document is worth
handing to a coaching system rather than filing.

Two further observations for anyone integrating this:

- **Running is optional by design.** Every run in the block has a non-running substitute, so an
  athlete can complete a full Fitstop week having run nothing at all. Any system that infers
  running volume from attendance will be wrong.
- **The 2 km time trial is a benchmark and not a run test.** Row, ski or bike. It is a genuine
  repeated measure worth tracking, and it must never be converted into a running pace.

## 5. How a coaching system should use this

1. **As a constraint, not a source.** These six days are fixed points in the athlete's week.
   Schedule around them; never schedule over them; never restate them as your own prescription.
2. **Program only the gap.** Aerobic running, threshold running and sled, in that order of
   value. Everything else is at least partially covered and adding to it buys fatigue rather
   than fitness.
3. **Do not stack.** Six hard days plus a full HYROX plan is how an athlete arrives at a start
   line already broken. If only one day is free, say plainly that one running session a week
   keeps a run honest and cannot build it, and let the athlete decide whether to trade a gym day.
4. **Respect the session ordering rule.** Where run quality has to share a day with a Lift
   session, the running goes first. Elite practice is to develop speed fresh and express it
   under fatigue (`PRA-0002`), and hard running on already-lifted legs is a worse session besides.
5. **Track the 2 km time trial** across weeks as a repeated measure. It is the only clean,
   dated, comparable number the gym week produces.
6. **Never grade this.** If a claim needs support, this document is not it. See `DR-0021`.
